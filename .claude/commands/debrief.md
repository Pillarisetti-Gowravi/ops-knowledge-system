## If $ARGUMENTS is empty

Ask the following questions **one at a time**, waiting for a response before asking the next:

1. "What did you accomplish today?"
2. "What conversations or interactions mattered — and why?"
3. "Did your priorities shift at all?"
4. "Any ideas, observations, or loose threads to capture?"

---

## Writing the log entry

After collecting answers (or processing $ARGUMENTS), write a structured entry to `wiki/log.md` using this exact format, appended at the end of the file:

```
| YYYY-MM-DD | debrief | — | — |

**Debrief — YYYY-MM-DD**
- [bullet 1]
- [bullet 2]
- [bullet 3]
(up to 5 bullets)
```

**Signal rules — apply these strictly:**
- Write only what shifted, what blocked, or what to carry forward.
- Do not transcribe answers. Compress. If the user said something that is context but not signal, leave it out.
- Each bullet should be a complete thought a future reader can act on or reference — not a summary of what was said.
- Use past tense for completed events, present tense for ongoing states.

---

## Wiki page updates

After writing the log entry:

- Scan the answers for mentions of existing wiki pages (by topic, person, or project name). If a match is found, offer to update those pages with any new signal. Ask before making changes.
- If something genuinely new surfaces — a concept, person, or project not yet in the wiki — propose creating a page for it. State what the page would cover and ask for confirmation before creating it.

---

## Priority shifts

If the user's answers suggest a priority has changed (a project stalled, a new one emerged, an area became more urgent), surface a specific suggested edit to `priorities.md` and ask for confirmation before writing it.

---

## End

Close with a single plain-English sentence summarizing the day — no header, no bullet, just one line. Make it specific to what actually came up, not generic.
